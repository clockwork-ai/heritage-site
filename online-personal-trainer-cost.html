    What an online personal trainercosts in the UK.

    Most UK online coaches charge somewhere between £60 and £200 a month. Premium one-to-one coaching sits above that. Here's what you get at each level, how it compares with paying a PT by the session, and what to ask before you hand over any money.

    Apply for 1:1 coaching →
    01

    The short answer

    Published UK guides put standard online coaching at roughly £60 to £200 a month (Trainera, Personal Trainer Cost UK, Oct 2026; GymGuide, Personal Trainer Cost UK 2026). App-only plans start lower. One-to-one coaching where the coach personally reads your data every week sits above that range. Mine works out at £67 to £113 a week, depending on the plan.

    An in-person personal trainer is usually priced by the session, at about £35 to £65 in a gym (GymGuide, Personal Trainer Cost UK 2026). In Stockport, where I'm based, Bark's listed average is £47 a session (Bark, Personal Trainers in Stockport).

    03

    What you're actually paying for

    Two coaches can both say "online coaching" and mean completely different things. The price usually follows five things:

    • Who reads your check-in. The coach, an assistant, or nobody. Ask directly.
    • How often the plan changes. A plan that's adjusted weekly against your numbers is a different service from one rewritten every eight weeks.
    • Nutrition. A macro target built from your real maintenance, or a generic meal plan.
    • Form feedback. Whether you can send videos of your lifts and get notes back, and how fast.
    • Response time. A direct line answered within a day, or a support inbox.
    04

    Where cheap gets expensive

    The cheapest option costs the most if you quit it in week three. People under-report what they eat by 47% on average (Lichtman et al., NEJM 1992), so a plan nobody checks tends to drift without anyone noticing. Self-monitoring is one of the most consistent predictors of weight loss across studies (Burke et al., J Am Diet Assoc 2011), and the people who lose the most log their food more often (Harvey et al., Obesity 2019).

    • The same PDF sent to everyone, with your name added.
    • A check-in form that never gets a written reply.
    • A long minimum contract with no way out.
    • Promises of a set number of kilos in a set number of weeks.

    06

    Five questions to ask any coach

    • Who reads my check-in, and when will I hear back?
    • How do you set my calories, and how often do you change them?
    • Can I send you videos of my lifts?
    • What happens if I stop progressing?
    • Can I leave early, and what do I owe if I do?

    A good coach answers all five without hesitating. You'll find my answers in the coaching terms, in plain English.

    ?

    Questions

    Is online personal training cheaper than a gym PT?

    Usually, yes. Standard online coaching is about £60 to £200 a month, while three in-person sessions a week costs roughly £450 to £850 a month. Online coaching also covers your food, steps and recovery, not just the hours in the gym.

    Why do some online coaches charge £300 or more a month?

    Mostly because of time. It's still usually less than seeing a PT in person twice a week. At that level the coach personally reviews your data every week and adjusts your plan, gives form feedback, and answers messages directly. Cheaper plans usually mean less of that, or none.

    Can I pay monthly?

    With most coaches, yes. Every one of my 1:1 packages has a monthly payment plan, spread across the whole programme rather than front-loaded.

    Are there contracts?

    It varies, so ask before you pay. With me you can stop at any time. Nothing further is owed beyond instalments already due, and there's a 14-day cooling-off period.
