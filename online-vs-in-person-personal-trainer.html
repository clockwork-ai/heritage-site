    Online or in-personpersonal trainer?

    An in-person PT covers the hours you're in the gym. An online coach covers the whole week around them. Neither is better for everyone. Here's how they compare and which one suits you.

    02

    When in-person is the better choice

    • You've never trained and need someone to teach you the lifts in person.
    • You're coming back from an injury and need hands-on supervision.
    • You only train when someone is physically waiting for you.
    • You enjoy it and the budget isn't a concern.
    03

    When online is the better choice

    • Your results stall because of food, sleep and weekends, not the sessions themselves.
    • Your week doesn't fit fixed appointments: shifts, kids, travel.
    • You can already train safely and want structure, progression and someone reading your data.
    • You want more support for less money than three sessions a week would cost.
    04

    Why the hours outside the gym matter more

    Most people overestimate what extra gym time does and underestimate food. Exercise-only programmes lost about 6kg less at a year than programmes combining diet and activity (Johns et al., J Acad Nutr Diet 2014). And people under-report what they eat by 47% on average (Lichtman et al., NEJM 1992). An hour with a trainer can't fix a week of guessed meals.

    That's the gap online coaching is built for. Three focused sessions a week, 10,000 steps a day, and macros hit most days, with someone checking all three every week.

    05

    Or do both

    They aren't mutually exclusive. A few in-person sessions to learn the main lifts, then online coaching to run the whole week, is a sensible route for a complete beginner. If you already train, you can usually skip straight to online.

    I'm an online coach based in Stockport, coaching men across the UK. If you want to see what that looks like week to week, the coaching page shows a real example check-in.

    Questions

    Is an online personal trainer as good as a real one?

    For most people who can already train safely, yes, and often better value, because online coaching covers food, steps and recovery as well as training. For a complete beginner, a few in-person sessions to learn technique first can help.

    How do online trainers check your form?

    You film your working sets and upload them. A good coach sends back timestamped notes on set-up, tempo and range. I aim to return video feedback within 48 hours.

    Which is cheaper?

    Online coaching, usually. Two in-person sessions a week costs roughly £70 to £130 a week in the UK. Standard online plans are about £60 to £200 a month, and one-to-one online coaching like mine is £67 to £113 a week, while covering the whole week rather than two hours of it.

    Can I have an online coach and still train at my gym?

    Yes. That's the normal set-up. Your programme is written for the gym you already use, and you log every session for review.
