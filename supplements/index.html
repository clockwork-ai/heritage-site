Tier 1 · the foundation

Buy these first

Strong evidence, cheap, and they do something you'll actually notice or measure. If your budget stops here, you've lost nothing that matters.

Creatine monohydrate

3 to 5 g every day

The most researched supplement in existence and the one that reliably does what it says: more work per session, better recovery between sets, a bit more size over time. Monohydrate only. The fancy versions cost more and do nothing extra.

When: any time of day, every day including rest days. No loading phase needed, it just takes 3 to 4 weeks to saturate. The 1 to 2kg on the scale in week one is water inside the muscle, not fat.
At Full Boar: Per4m Micronized Creatine, 50 servings, about £15. Cheapest thing on this page and the one I'd never skip.

Whey protein

25 g per serving

Not magic, just convenient protein. It exists so hitting your daily target isn't a battle on busy days. 25 g is the dose that actually does the job in the research, so don't skimp with a half scoop.

When: whenever it plugs a gap. Around training is convenient, not compulsory. Total daily protein is what matters.
At Full Boar: Per4m Whey (900 g or 2 kg) and Naughty Boy Advanced Whey. Buy on flavour and price per kg, they're all much of a muchness.

Vitamin D3

2,000 to 5,000 IU daily

The most common deficiency there is, and we live in the UK, so assume you're low from about October to April. Affects mood, immune function and hormonal health. This is the one worth actually testing rather than guessing.

When: daily with a meal containing fat. Ask your GP for a vitamin D blood test if you've never had one.
At Full Boar: HR Labs D3 5000iu, or Supplement Needs D3+K2 if you want the K2 alongside it.

Omega-3 (EPA + DHA)

2 to 3 g combined

Anti-inflammatory, good for blood lipids, blood pressure and mood. Check the label properly: you want 2 to 3 g of EPA + DHA combined, not 2 to 3 g of "fish oil", which is usually a much smaller dose hiding in a bigger capsule.

When: daily with food. Skip it if you're already eating oily fish several times a week.
At Full Boar: Supplement Needs Omega-3 softgels, or TBJP Oh-Mega if you want a bigger tub.

Magnesium

200 to 400 mg evening

Involved in sleep quality, muscle function and stress. Hard training raises what you need and most people eat less than they think. Form matters: bisglycinate, threonate or citrate. Avoid oxide, it's cheap for a reason and mostly ends up in the toilet.

When: evening, an hour or so before bed.
At Full Boar: Supplement Needs Magnesium Bisglycinate. That's the right form, avoid the ZMA-style blends if magnesium is what you're actually after.
Tier 2 · training performance

Once the basics are in

These make sessions better rather than making you healthier. Worth it if you train hard and consistently. Pointless if tier 1 and your sleep aren't sorted.

Caffeine

200 to 400 mg pre-session

The most effective legal performance drug we have. Roughly 3 to 6 mg per kg of bodyweight before a session, which for most people is 200 to 400 mg. Coffee counts.

When: 45 to 60 minutes before training, and not within 8 hours of bed. Keep everyday intake lower than your pre-session dose so it still works when you need it. Trading sleep for a better session is a bad deal.
Not sold on its own there and it doesn't need to be. Coffee is cheaper, or it's already in any stim pre-workout you buy.

Citrulline malate

6 to 8 g pre-session

More blood flow, better work capacity, and yes, a proper pump. One of the few pre-workout ingredients that earns its place at a full dose. Most cheap pre-workouts contain a fraction of what's needed.

When: about an hour before lifting.
At Full Boar: easiest as a properly dosed pre-workout rather than a standalone tub. Per4m Pump (stim free), PER4M Pre Workout STIM, or TBJP Pumpage. Check the label lists at least 6 g.

Beta-alanine

3 to 5 g daily

Buffers the burn in longer sets, so it helps most in the 60-second-plus range and does little for heavy triples. Takes about 4 weeks of daily use to build up, so timing it around a session is irrelevant.

When: any time, daily. The face and hand tingling is harmless. Split the dose if you'd rather avoid it.
Comes inside most pre-workouts rather than on its own. If you want the daily dose properly, a plain standalone tub from anywhere is fine, it's a commodity.

Electrolytes

as needed

Sodium and potassium, not a sports drink full of sugar. Genuinely useful if you're a heavy sweater, training in heat, on a low-carb phase, or getting cramps and light-headedness in sessions. Otherwise food covers it.

When: during long or sweaty sessions. Salt your food properly first, it's cheaper.
At Full Boar: Supplement Needs Electrolyte Plus. Or a pinch of salt in your water, which is free and works.
Tier 3 · sleep & stress

The recovery end

Optional, and only worth touching once your sleep routine itself is fixed. A supplement won't out-run a phone in bed at midnight.

Ashwagandha (KSM-66)

500 to 600 mg pre-bed

An adaptogen with decent evidence for lowering stress and cortisol, and people generally report sleeping better on it. Look for a standardised extract such as KSM-66 rather than raw powder.

When: with your last meal or before bed. Worth cycling: a few months on, a few weeks off, as the effect can fade.
At Full Boar: Supplement Needs KSM66, Per4m KSM66 or Strom KSM66. All the right extract, buy on price.

L-theanine

100 to 200 mg

Takes the edge off without sedating you. Pairs well with caffeine to smooth out the jittery bit, and works pre-bed if your head is still racing.

When: with your pre-workout caffeine, or an hour before bed. Not both.
Usually only found inside sleep or nootropic blends. Standalone is cheap anywhere, no need to be fussy about brand.

Glycine

3 g pre-bed

Cheap, mild, and some decent evidence for falling asleep faster and feeling sharper the next morning. Low-risk experiment if sleep onset is your issue.

When: 30 to 60 minutes before bed.
Not a standalone line there. Any plain glycine powder does the job.
On melatonin: you'll see it recommended constantly online, but in the UK it is prescription-only, so it isn't on this list and I won't be advising you on doses of it. If your sleep is genuinely broken, that's a conversation with your GP, and a conversation with me about your training and stress load.
Save your money

What I'd skip

  • BCAAs and EAAs. If you're hitting your daily protein, they add nothing. The research is clear that whole protein beats individual amino acids, and you're already paying for it in your whey.
  • Fat burners. Caffeine plus a marketing budget. The rest of the label is dosed too low to matter, and none of them touch what a calorie deficit does.
  • Testosterone boosters. If they worked as advertised they'd be prescription medication. Fix your sleep, bodyfat, training and zinc and vitamin D status instead, that's the actual lever.
  • Mass gainers. A very expensive way to buy maltodextrin. Eat the extra food, or blend oats and peanut butter into your whey.
  • Fancy creatine forms. HCl, ethyl ester, buffered, all of it. Monohydrate wins on evidence and costs a fraction.
  • Detox teas and greens powders. Your liver and kidneys already do the job. Eat 500 g of actual fruit and veg a day instead, which is the single best thing on this whole page and costs nothing extra.
If you're starting from nothing

Buy it in this order

1
Creatine + vitamin D3

Under a tenner a month between them. Best value on the entire page.

2
Whey protein

Only if hitting your protein target is genuinely hard. If food gets you there, skip it.

3
Omega-3 + magnesium

The health and recovery layer. You'll feel the magnesium in your sleep within a fortnight if you were low.

4
Caffeine + citrulline

Only once you're training hard enough for a better session to be worth paying for.

5
Everything else

Optional, situational, and honestly never the reason someone does or doesn't get results.

The honest summary: creatine, vitamin D and protein do most of the work on this page. Everything else is a rounding error next to eating enough protein, sleeping seven-plus hours and turning up to your sessions. If you're ever choosing between a supplement order and a food shop, buy the food.
