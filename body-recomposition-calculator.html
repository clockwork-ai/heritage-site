Body Recomposition Calculator: lose fat and build muscle.

A recomp runs at maintenance calories with high protein, and lets the training do the work. Add your body fat % and see your calories and protein straight away. The macro split and how to run it come with your name and email.

Why I need it: your macros are worked out from it. Two men can both weigh 95kg, but if one is carrying a lot more fat, setting protein off his full weight runs it far too high. At 15% body fat or under, I use your full weight. From 25% up, I use your lean mass. In between, it shifts gradually from one to the other, so there's no sudden jump.

Not sure? How to estimate it
  • Smart scale: weigh first thing in the morning, after the toilet and before food or drink. It is a rough read, so treat it as a guide.
  • Tape measure: use the tape method on the TDEE calculator. Neck and waist, about two minutes.
  • Mirror check (men, rough guide): abs clearly showing, roughly 10 to 14%. Flat stomach with a faint ab outline in good light, roughly 15 to 19%. Soft stomach with no outline, roughly 20 to 25%. Stomach over the belt, 25% and up.
  • Women: add roughly 8 to 10 points to those mirror ranges.
  • Stuck between two? Pick the higher one. Guessing low pushes your numbers up.

How it works

How I coach a recomp.

Maintenance calories, high protein, and training that keeps progressing. Here is the whole method in plain English.

What is body recomposition?

Losing fat and building muscle at the same time. Because one goes down while the other goes up, the scale barely moves. So you judge a recomp on your waist and your lifts, not your weight.

How many calories should I eat for a recomp?

Maintenance. Not a deficit, not a surplus. The training and the protein drive the change. The calculator gives you a starting estimate. Your true maintenance comes from 2 to 3 weeks of a stable weekly average weight. If your weight drifts more than about 0.5 kg either way, adjust your steps first and your food second.

How much protein do I need for a recomp?

High: around 2 to 2.2 g per kg. The calculator works that out from a reference weight, the same way as my other calculators. At 15% body fat or under it uses your full weight. From 25% up it uses your lean mass. In between, it blends from one to the other. Keep most of your carbs around your training.

What training do I need to do?

Double progression: work up the reps in a set range, then add weight and start again at the bottom of the range. If your lifts are not going up, it is not a recomp. It is just maintenance.

How do I know if my recomp is working?

Your weekly average weight stays within about 1 kg. Your waist comes down slowly or holds. Rep PRs keep coming. Take photos every 4 weeks. Keep your steps and cardio fixed at a set level, so they do not hide what is happening.

How long does a recomp take?

Judge it in blocks of 8 to 12 weeks, never week to week. A single week tells you almost nothing when the scale is meant to stay still.

Who is a recomp good for?

Beginners, people coming back after time off, men on TRT, and anyone carrying extra fat with a decent training base. It is slow for a trained lifter who is already lean. If that is you, a fat loss or muscle gain phase from the TDEE calculator will move faster.
