Why I need it: your target is worked out from it. Two men can both weigh 95kg, but if one is carrying a lot more fat, setting protein off his full weight runs it far too high. At 15% body fat or under, I use your full weight. From 25% up, I use your lean mass. In between, it shifts gradually from one to the other, so there's no sudden jump.

Not sure? How to estimate it
  • Smart scale: weigh first thing in the morning, after the toilet and before food or drink. It is a rough read, so treat it as a guide.
  • Tape measure: use the tape method on my TDEE calculator. Neck and waist, about two minutes.
  • Mirror check (men, rough guide): abs clearly showing, roughly 10 to 14%. Flat stomach with a faint ab outline in good light, roughly 15 to 19%. Soft stomach with no outline, roughly 20 to 25%. Stomach over the belt, 25% and up.
  • Women: add roughly 8 to 10 points to those mirror ranges.
  • Stuck between two? Pick the higher one. Guessing low pushes your numbers up.

Resistance sessions to preserve muscle
Hitting this is the hard part. When appetite drops, protein is the first thing to fall, and that’s exactly when your body starts taking the shortfall from muscle. Front-load protein early in the day, make every meal protein-first, and lean on high-protein, low-volume foods.
Why this matters: when you lose weight without a system, research shows roughly a quarter to nearly 40% of what you lose can be muscle, not fat. Combine enough protein with resistance training and that can drop to around 15 to 20%, meaning you keep the strength, shape and metabolism you’re working for, and you’re far more likely to hold the result long-term.

What to do next

  1. Hit the daily number above, split across the meals you will actually eat.
  2. If you are losing weight, stay in the fat-loss range. If you are eating to build, use the muscle-gain figure.
  3. Protein at every meal is the first of the Five Foundations. The guide also covers steps, three short sessions, sleep, and a weekly check-in.

This calculator provides general nutrition and training guidance, not medical advice. Protein needs vary with health status and kidney function. If you’re losing weight with the support of any medication, always follow your prescriber’s instructions and speak to your healthcare provider before making changes to your diet or training. Targets follow the J3U coaching method: 1.3 to 1.5 g of protein per lb of body weight (about 2.9 to 3.3 g per kg) in a fat loss phase, 1.0 to 1.3 g per lb (about 2.2 to 2.9 g per kg) when building, and 0.4 to 0.65 g per kg per meal across 4 to 6 meals. Body fat % is required. At 15% body fat or under, targets use total body weight. From 25% up, they use lean mass (fat-free mass). In between, they blend gradually from one to the other.
