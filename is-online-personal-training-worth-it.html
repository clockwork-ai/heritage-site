Structure over motivation

    Guide · updated October 2026

    Is online personal trainingworth it?

    It's worth it if you'll track honestly and a real coach reads your numbers every week. It isn't if you're after motivation, or a PDF you'll stop opening by week three. Here's how to tell which one you're buying.

    Apply for 1:1 coaching →
    01

    The honest answer

    Online coaching works when it does two things: makes you measure what you're doing, and puts someone in charge of reading those measurements and changing the plan. Take either away and you're paying for a programme you could have found for free.

    That's not my opinion. Across 22 studies, self-monitoring was consistently linked to weight loss (Burke et al., J Am Diet Assoc 2011). People who lost 10% or more of their weight logged food 2.7 times a day, against 1.7 for those who lost less (Harvey et al., Obesity 2019). The tracking is the work. The coach makes sure it gets done, and gets used.

    02

    It's worth it when

    • You've started more than once and keep stalling around the same point.
    • You know roughly what to do, but not what to change when it stops working.
    • Your week is busy and unpredictable: work, kids, shifts, travel.
    • You'll log your food and training honestly, even on the bad days.
    • You want to learn to run it yourself eventually, not depend on someone forever.
    03

    It isn't worth it when

    • You want someone to physically stand next to you every session.
    • You won't track what you eat. Without the data, there's nothing to coach.
    • You want a six-week shred before a holiday, then back to normal.
    • Nobody actually reads your check-in. Then it's just an expensive app.

    If you're a complete beginner who's never been in a gym, a few in-person sessions to learn the lifts can be money well spent first. I've written about that in online versus in-person training.

    04

    The part most plans skip

    Losing weight isn't the hard bit. Keeping it is. Five years after structured weight-loss programmes, people had kept only 17 to 29% of what they lost on average (Anderson et al., Am J Clin Nutr 2001). That's why the measure of good coaching isn't the first eight weeks. It's whether you leave knowing how to run the system without the coach.

    That's also why I don't sell endless cardio. Six to twelve months of cardio alone produced about 1.6kg of weight loss across 14 trials (Thorogood et al., Am J Med 2011). Food, three lifting sessions a week and daily steps do the heavy lifting.

    05

    What it looks like in practice

    • Mark, 44, tiler and dad. Waist down 28cm in nine weeks, 119cm to 91cm, measured by him at the navel every week. Chest up 6cm.
    • Dale. Blood pressure 180/126 in hospital, 114/74 ten weeks in, and 8.7kg down. His own readings, from his own post. Medication decisions stayed with his GP.
    • Jonny. Over ten years of trying on his own. Eight weeks in: "I'm feeling and looking the best I ever have."
    • Me. Stage 2 hypertension, 151/105 in January 2023, to 113/69 in March 2024 on the same monitor, with no medication.

    All coached online. More on the results page. Individual results: yours depend on where you start and how consistently you follow the plan.

    06

    How to know within eight weeks

    You shouldn't have to take anyone's word for it. Eight weeks of honest tracking is enough to see whether something is moving: the scale, your waist, your measurements, your sleep or your eating habits.

    That's what my 8-Week Progress Guarantee is built on. Hold 80% or more on your weekly structure score for the first eight weeks. If none of those has improved, the next month is on me. The score measures whether you did the work, so if you did and nothing moved, that's on the plan, and fixing it is my job.

    Questions

    Does online personal training actually work?

    It can, when it makes you track honestly and a coach reads that data and adjusts your plan. Self-monitoring is one of the most consistent predictors of weight loss in the research. A plan nobody checks tends to drift.

    How much does online personal training cost in the UK?

    Typically £60 to £200 a month for standard online plans. One-to-one coaching where the coach personally reviews everything weekly costs more, though usually less than seeing a PT in person twice a week. The cost guide has the full breakdown.

    How quickly will I see results?

    Most people see something move within eight weeks of consistent, honest tracking: weight, waist, measurements, sleep or eating habits. Lasting change takes months, which is why longer plans exist.

    Is online coaching good for beginners?

    Yes, as long as you get proper form feedback. Send videos of your lifts and get notes back. If you've never set foot in a gym, a few in-person sessions to learn the basics can help too.
